SHEET 01RESEARCH2023 — PRESENT

Research

My research trajectory runs from post‑flood shelter to a broader question: what should a planning system do when spatial data, institutional knowledge and community knowledge give different accounts of climate risk?

Independent research · 2025 — present

Human–Hydro Inference (HHI)

An experimental framework testing whether an AI system can recognise the limits of what sparse flood and settlement evidence supports. Using real Pakistan Bureau of Statistics and NDMA/PDMA records for the 2022–23 floods, HHI separates deterministic spatial computation from model interpretation and checks every AI‑generated claim against an independent rule‑based validator.

  • Distinguishes directly supported evidence, reasonable derivation, structurally unanswerable questions and unsupported premises.
  • Twenty named failure modes — e.g. treating a missing value as zero, or a camp headcount as confirmed displacement.
  • Develops epistemic restraint: “the evidence does not establish this” counts as a correct output.
HHI Atlas — code & build reports

Master’s thesis · NUST · 2023 — 2024

Flood recovery & transitional shelter, Rajanpur

Understanding Prototype Structures for Transitional Shelter for Rural Flooding. Mixed‑methods research combining household surveys with community and stakeholder perspectives on post‑flood shelter: functionality, privacy, comfort, cultural fit, durability, livelihoods and mobility.

Key lesson: two households exposed to the same flood can follow very different recovery paths — the spatial outcome is inseparable from social and institutional conditions.

Interactive flood maps

Two research sites built on one verified data chain. Drop two circles on the map and compare who lived in the flood path. Every number on the page is read from a sourced facts file, and every limit is stated.

Pakistan · 2022 · Live site ↗

When the Indus rose

Satellite‑detected floodwater (UNOSAT / VIIRS), WorldPop population, OpenStreetMap buildings and 2017 tehsil census, with modelled 1‑in‑10, 100 and 500‑year river‑flood scenarios.

≈ 33.2 million modelled residents within the observed 2022 flood extent (86,641 km²).

A first pass nobody has checked on the ground; cloud, tree cover or dense housing can hide water.

Netherlands · 2021 · Live site ↗

Who lived in the flood path, Limburg

CBS 100 m population grid, BAG buildings, the 4TU observed flood extent and national dike‑breach scenarios (1‑in‑10 to 1‑in‑10,000 years), with AHN4 terrain, land use and soil.

6,535 residents in 100 m cells inside the observed extent, of 282,760 in the study area.

The observed extent is preliminary and partly modelled; 2021 buildings are reconstructed from today’s BAG.

Source code: indus-flood-2022 · limburg-flood-2021 · hhi-atlas

SHEET 03GIS MAP PLATESQGIS · PYTHON · 2026

Map plates

Seven reproducible spatial studies. Each one starts from a research question, runs on open data through a scripted QGIS/Python pipeline, and reports associations only — with provenance, thresholds and limits documented. Click a plate to see the full board.

P01 · Pakistan, 2022

Where the 2022 flood met the settled ground

Where did the mapped 2022 flood extent coincide with buildings, people and roads in the Larkana–Shikarpur–Jacobabad corridor (13,092 km²)?

Sentinel‑1 flood extent (TU Wien, 14 dates) · Microsoft building footprints · WorldPop 2020 · OSM roads · Copernicus GLO‑30

  • 7,884 km² detected flood — 67.7% of observable ground
  • 74,564 mapped buildings intersect detected flood (a floor)
  • 90.2% of modelled population in 1 km cells ≥10% flooded
  • 39% of buildings sit on ground the satellite never observed — the largest evidence gap lies under the settlement

P02 · Athens, Greece

Thermal Athens

How does summer land‑surface temperature relate to built intensity, vegetation and population across Attica?

Satellite land‑surface temperature (summer median) · built intensity · vegetation · population

Seven sequential analytical states, from base city to final overlay of the hottest areas.

P03 · Dhaka, Bangladesh

Dhaka — the city leans north

How has rapid urban expansion (2000–2020) met land that is wet, low‑lying or both?

GHS‑BUILT‑S R2023A · JRC Global Surface Water · SRTM

  • +125 km² (+51%) built footprint
  • 64% of growth to the north and north‑west
  • 11% of new footprint on constrained land
